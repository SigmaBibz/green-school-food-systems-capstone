# References and evidence notes

## Verified external references

1. World Wildlife Fund (WWF). [*How much food is wasted in schools, and what can we do about it?*](https://www.worldwildlife.org/resources/explainers/how-much-food-is-wasted-in-schools-and-what-can-we-do-about-it/)

   WWF estimates that U.S. schools waste 530,000 tons of solid food annually, excluding milk, with an estimated value of $1.7 billion. Attribute these figures to WWF and describe them as estimates, not precise current measurements or results from the proposed pilot.

2. World Wildlife Fund (WWF). (2025). [*Food Waste Fact Sheet*](https://files.worldwildlife.org/wwfcmsprod/files/Publication/file/611b23d66z_2025_FWW_Fact_Sheet_250822.pdf)

   Reports the estimate of 530,000 tons of school food waste and approximately 39 pounds of food waste per student per year, drawing on WWF Food Waste Warriors research. The 39-pound figure is per student per year, not per school per day.

3. U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA). (2020). [*Wasted Food Measurement Methodology Scoping Memo*](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2020-06/documents/food_measurement_methodology_scoping_memo-6-18-20.pdf)

   Estimates approximately 1.2 million tons of food waste generated by K–12 schools in 2016 using EPA's stated methodology. This is a separate estimate from WWF's; keep each figure attributed to its source, year, scope, and method rather than combining them as directly comparable measurements.

## Evidence and limitations

- The 530,000-ton and $1.7 billion figures are WWF estimates, not direct measurements from the proposed pilot schools. The 530,000-ton estimate refers to solid food waste and excludes milk.
- The original capstone's claim that an average school discards 39 pounds of food and 19 gallons of milk daily is not supported as written. WWF's 2025 fact sheet describes approximately 39 pounds per student per year; the 19-gallons-of-milk-per-day claim remains unverified and should be removed unless its original source is found.
- The original claim that students discard 27–53% of food served needs a specific, traceable study and its methodology. Do not repeat it without that evidence.
- The 25% food-waste reduction figure in the portfolio report and the original capstone's 30% food-waste reduction are proposed targets, not measured results. The original 40% digital-emissions and 50% digital-carbon reduction claims have no documented baseline or calculation and should not be used.
- A proposed 40% improvement in webpage loading speed is a performance target to test; it is not evidence of lower emissions.
- Assess digital efficiency using measurable indicators such as page weight, data transferred, number of requests, and synchronization frequency. Any digital-emissions savings claim requires a defensible baseline, calculation method, and documented assumptions.
- Verify hosting-provider energy and renewable-energy claims directly with the provider before relying on them in an environmental assessment.
